What is [Umbral](https://github.com/Cosmic-Chimps/umbral)?

Umbral was born from the mantra _authentication is hard, don't build it yourself_. With that mantra in my head, every time I started a _toy project_ I had to choose how to do authentication, and the headache began:

- I added social login by hand
- I used [Auth0](https://auth0.com/), [Clerk](https://clerk.com/)
- I used [Keycloak](https://www.keycloak.org/), [Zitadel](https://zitadel.com/)
- I used local users, with their passwords and everything that comes with them

I've made quite a few _toy projects_ and gone through so many ways of doing authentication, and all of it was boilerplate. If I added social login, I had to go to GitHub, to Google, to Discord, create the keys on each site and wire it all up in each project's code. And on the next project, the same thing all over again.

I could have had one Keycloak or one Zitadel for all my _toy projects_. But those are whole platforms, with realms, roles and flows, for something I solved with a "Sign in with GitHub" button. I didn't need almost any of it.

Looking for something smaller I found [Dex](https://dexidp.io/), and it came close. But if a friend signed in one day with GitHub and another day with Google, to my app they were two different people. Dex has no list of users I can see or disable, and everything is configured in a YAML file you edit and then restart. Dex is great at what it was made for, which is putting a login in front of a cluster or a company's tools. I wanted something else: a small place where my users live, wherever they come from.

That's how [Umbral](https://github.com/Cosmic-Chimps/umbral) was born, and here is the [documentation](https://cosmic-chimps.github.io/umbral/).

The idea is simple. Before, I created each provider's keys for each project and wired them up in each app. Now I set them up once in Umbral, and every new app only needs three values: _the issuer URL_, a _Client ID_ and a _Secret_. Any normal OpenID Connect library will do.

Today you can sign in with eight providers: [Discord](https://cosmic-chimps.github.io/umbral/connectors/discord/), [GitHub](https://cosmic-chimps.github.io/umbral/connectors/github/), [Google](https://cosmic-chimps.github.io/umbral/connectors/google/), [Apple](https://cosmic-chimps.github.io/umbral/connectors/apple/), [Microsoft](https://cosmic-chimps.github.io/umbral/connectors/microsoft/), the [Fediverse](https://cosmic-chimps.github.io/umbral/connectors/fediverse/) (Mastodon, GoToSocial), [Bluesky](https://cosmic-chimps.github.io/umbral/connectors/bluesky/) and any OpenID Connect provider you already have (Authentik, Keycloak, GitLab, Forgejo…).

And with passkeys too 😃. A friend told me "I don't use social login, add FIDO", I said "what a good idea", and now we have passkeys. So a passkey in Umbral isn't a second step: it's the whole login, with no account anywhere else.

What mattered to me:

- One container with SQLite inside. No Postgres, no Redis. And it should run on a small VM or a Raspberry Pi.
- Three concepts and that's it: Apps, Connectors and Users.
- One person, one user: if they sign in with GitHub and later with Google, to your apps they are still the same person.
- What it doesn't do, on purpose: no roles, no MFA, no passwords. It answers "who is this person?" and nothing more. What that person is allowed to do is your app's business.

If you use .NET, there's also an [Aspire integration](https://cosmic-chimps.github.io/umbral/run/aspire/), and there are [samples](https://github.com/Cosmic-Chimps/umbral/tree/main/samples) for several frameworks and languages: ASP.NET Core, FastAPI, Next.js, a SPA, Android, iOS and .NET MAUI.

Honestly, I'm happy with Umbral. I'm using it in Frunchy, and so far none of my friends have had trouble signing up or signing in.

Umbral is written in F# with [Oxpecker](https://lanayx.github.io/Oxpecker/).

Cheers.
